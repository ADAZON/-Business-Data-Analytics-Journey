- Understanding business problems
- Asking relevant questions

- Communicating findings clearly
- Recommending practical business actions

The real value of a Business Data Analyst comes from the ability to connect data with business needs.

## 2.1 Understanding Data and Insight

One of the first concepts studied was the difference between data and insight.

### Data

Data consists of raw facts, figures, observations, measurements, or records collected for examination.

Examples include:

- Sales figures
- Customer records
- Product prices
- Website visits
- Order quantities
- Delivery times

### Insight

An insight is a meaningful conclusion drawn from data that improves understanding or supports decision-making.

The key lesson was that data alone does not automatically create business value.

Data must be:

1. Collected
2. Prepared
3. Analysed
4. Interpreted
5. Communicated clearly
6. Connected to a business decision

> Data becomes valuable when it helps stakeholders understand a situation and take appropriate action.

---

## 2.2 Application of Business Data Analytics

I examined how Business Data Analysts create value in different industries.

### E-commerce

In e-commerce, analysts may examine:

- Sales performance
- Customer behaviour
- Product performance
- Website traffic
- Conversion rates
- Customer retention
- Marketing campaigns
- Inventory availability

The purpose is to identify opportunities to improve revenue, customer experience, operational efficiency, and profitability.

### Healthcare

In healthcare, analysts may examine:

- Patient data
- Service delivery
- Operational performance
- Costs
- Waiting times
- Resource utilisation
- Treatment outcomes

The purpose is to improve service quality, reduce inefficiencies, control costs, and support evidence-based decisions.

This exercise showed that Business Data Analytics can be applied across industries because almost every organisation generates data and needs evidence to support decisions.

## 2.3 Importance of Data Visualisation

I learned that technically correct analysis can still fail if the findings are presented through confusing or inappropriate charts.

Stakeholders must be able to understand:

- What happened
- Where it happened
- When it happened
- Why it may have happened
- What action should be considered

Poor visualisation may result in:

- Misinterpretation
- Weak decisions
- Confusion
- Failure to identify important patterns
- No action being taken

Therefore, analytical accuracy and clear communication must work together.

> Insights only create value when they are communicated clearly.

# 3. Framework for Approaching an Ambiguous Business Problem

During Week 1, I developed a framework for investigating business problems where the cause is not immediately clear.

## Step 1: Clarify the Problem

The analyst must distinguish between:

- The visible symptom
- The possible causes
- The actual business problem

For example, declining sales are a symptom. The cause may be poor marketing, low website traffic, stock shortages, pricing changes, operational failures, or customer dissatisfaction.

## Step 2: Define the Business Objective

The analyst must establish what the organisation wants to achieve.

Examples include:

- Increase revenue
- Improve conversion
- Reduce customer churn
- Improve operational efficiency
- Increase profitability
- Reduce delivery delays

## Step 3: Identify Stakeholders

Relevant stakeholders may include:

- Chief Executive Officer
- Sales Manager
- Marketing Manager
- Operations Manager
- Finance Manager
- Product Manager
- Customer Service Team

## Step 4: Identify the Required Data

The analyst must determine which datasets, measures, and business records are required.

## Step 5: Collect, Assess, and Clean the Data

The data must be reviewed for:

- Missing values
- Duplicates
- Inconsistent formatting
- Incorrect data types
- Unexpected values
- Incomplete records

## Step 6: Analyse Patterns and Possible Causes

The analyst should investigate:

- Trends
- Differences
- Relationships
- Anomalies
- Strong and weak performance
- Possible root causes

## Step 7: Communicate the Findings

The results should be presented using clear:

- Tables
- Charts
- Dashboards
- Written explanations
- Recommendations

## Step 8: Recommend and Monitor Actions

The analyst should recommend practical actions and define KPIs for measuring whether those actions work.

This framework reinforced that an analyst should not stop at explaining what happened.

A Business Data Analyst should also investigate why it happened, recommend what should happen next, and monitor the outcome.

# 4. Week 1 Assignment: Investigating Declining E-commerce Sales

## Business Scenario

The CEO of an e-commerce company reported that sales were declining.

The assignment required me to:

1. Identify the data that should be analysed
2. Develop questions to ask the CEO
3. Avoid making unsupported assumptions
4. Propose an analytical approach for identifying the cause

---

## 4.1 Business Problem

The reported symptom was declining sales.

However, the actual cause was unknown.

It would therefore be inappropriate to immediately conclude that the problem was caused by:

- Poor marketing
- High prices
- Competitors
- Low product quality
- Website problems
- Customer dissatisfaction

The correct analytical approach was to collect evidence and establish business context before drawing conclusions.

---

## 4.2 Data Required for the Investigation

I identified seven major categories of data.

---

### A. Sales Data

The following sales metrics would be analysed:

- Daily revenue
- Weekly revenue
- Monthly revenue
- Total orders
- Units sold
- Average Order Value
- Sales by product
- Sales by category
- Sales by brand
- Sales by region

### Business Purpose

This data would help determine:

- When the decline started
- Whether the decline was gradual or sudden
- Which products or categories were affected
- Whether the decline occurred in every region
- Whether customers were placing fewer orders
- Whether order values had decreased

---

### B. Customer Data

The following customer metrics would be analysed:

- New customers
- Returning customers
- Customer retention rate
- Purchase frequency
- Customer Lifetime Value

### Business Purpose

This analysis would help determine whether the decline resulted from:

- Weak customer acquisition
- Poor retention
- Reduced repeat purchases
- Lower customer value
- Reduced purchase frequency

---

### C. Website Analytics

The following website metrics would be examined:

- Website traffic
- Conversion rate
- Bounce rate
- Cart abandonment rate
- Checkout abandonment rate

### Business Purpose

This data would show whether:

- Fewer people were visiting the website
- Visitors were leaving quickly
- Customers were adding products to carts but not purchasing
- Customers were abandoning the checkout process
- Website traffic was not converting into sales

---

### D. Marketing Data

The following marketing measures would be examined:

- Traffic sources
- Advertising expenditure
- Campaign performance
- Email marketing metrics
- Marketing Return on Investment

### Business Purpose

This analysis would help evaluate whether:

- Marketing was reaching the right audience
- Advertising expenditure had changed
- Campaign performance had declined
- Email campaigns were effective
- Marketing activities were generating acceptable returns

---

### E. Product and Inventory Data

The following product and inventory information would be reviewed:

- Best-performing products
- Worst-performing products
- Stock availability
- Pricing changes
- Customer reviews

### Business Purpose

This data would help determine whether the sales decline was caused by:

- Reduced product demand
- Stock shortages
- Pricing problems
- Poor customer reviews
- Product quality issues
- An unsuitable product mix

---

### F. Operational Data

The following operational measures would be examined:

- Shipping times
- Delivery performance
- Order cancellations
- Product return rates

### Business Purpose

This analysis would help identify whether operational problems were reducing customer satisfaction or discouraging repeat purchases.

For example:

- Slow delivery may increase cancellations
- Poor delivery performance may reduce trust
- High return rates may indicate product or fulfilment problems

---

### G. Financial Data

The following financial measures would be reviewed:

- Gross profit
- Discounts
- Refunds
- Product margins

### Business Purpose

Revenue alone does not provide a complete picture of business health.

Sales may increase while profitability declines due to:

- Excessive discounts
- High refunds
- Low-margin products
- Increased operational costs

Therefore, revenue and profitability should be analysed together.

---

# 5. Questions for the CEO

Before beginning the analysis, I developed questions to establish the business context.

1. When was the decline in sales first noticed?
2. Is the decline affecting every product or only specific categories?
3. Have there been recent changes to prices, promotions, or discounts?
4. Has the marketing budget or advertising strategy changed?
5. Have there been website updates or technical problems affecting purchases?
6. Have customers complained about product quality, delivery, or customer service?
7. Have competitors introduced new products, promotions, or pricing strategies?
8. Have there been inventory shortages or supply-chain disruptions?
9. Is the decline occurring across all regions or only in specific markets?
10. Have recent business decisions or external events affected sales?

These questions would help establish:

- Timing
- Scale
- Business context
- Possible causes
- Areas requiring deeper investigation

---

# 6. Proposed Analytical Outcome

After collecting the required data and obtaining contextual information, the analysis would aim to:

1. Identify the likely root causes of declining sales
2. Present the findings through appropriate charts, dashboards, and explanations
3. Recommend evidence-based actions
4. Establish KPIs for monitoring improvement
5. Assess whether the recommended actions produced the expected results

Possible causes could include:

- Weak customer retention
- Stock shortages
- Pricing changes
- Ineffective marketing
- Delivery problems

- Increased competition

The assignment demonstrated that business problems should be investigated through evidence rather than assumptions.

# 8. Data Analytics, Data Science, and Business Intelligence

## Data Analytics

### Main Focus

Examining data to answer questions, identify patterns, investigate performance, and generate business insights.

### Common Tools

- Excel
- SQL
- Power BI
- Tableau
- Python

### Typical Outcomes

- Findings
- Reports
- Business recommendations
- Performance analysis

---

## Data Science

### Main Focus

Using advanced statistics, programming, machine learning, and modelling to solve complex problems.

### Common Tools

- Python
- R
- SQL
- Machine-learning tools

### Typical Outcomes

- Predictive models
- Algorithms
- Automation
- Data products

---

## Business Intelligence

### Main Focus

Organising and presenting business information for monitoring and decision-making.

### Common Tools

- Power BI
- Tableau
- SQL
- Excel
- Data warehouses

### Typical Outcomes

- Dashboards
- Reports
- KPI monitoring
- Performance-management systems

---

## Key Distinction

Although the three fields overlap:

- Data Analytics investigates and interprets data
- Data Science focuses more heavily on advanced prediction and modelling
- Business Intelligence focuses on reporting, dashboards, and organisational performance monitoring

# 12. Key Lessons Learned

## Lesson 1: Business Understanding Comes First

The analyst must understand the business problem before selecting tools or performing calculations.

## Lesson 2: Data Is Not the Same as Insight

Raw values only become useful when they are interpreted and connected to a decision.

## Lesson 3: Analysts Must Avoid Unsupported Assumptions

Possible causes should be tested with evidence rather than accepted as facts.

## Lesson 4: Communication Is a Core Analytical Skill

Even accurate findings can fail to create value if stakeholders cannot understand them.

## Lesson 5: Technical and Soft Skills Must Work Together

Tools help perform the analysis, but critical thinking, curiosity, business understanding, and communication determine whether the analysis creates value.

## Lesson 6: The Analyst Must Go Beyond “What Happened?”

A complete analysis should address:

- What happened?
- Where did it happen?
- When did it happen?
- Why might it have happened?
- What should the business do next?
- How will success be measured?
